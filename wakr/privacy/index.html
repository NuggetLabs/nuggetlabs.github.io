WAKR Privacy Policy

Effective October 6, 2026

WAKR is an alarm clock app for Android, made by NuggetLabs. This policy explains what information WAKR uses, where it's kept, and what control you have over it. It covers the WAKR app and this website.

In short: WAKR doesn't collect your personal information. Everything the app records stays on your phone. The camera check runs entirely on your phone: nothing is recorded, and no image is ever sent to us or to anyone else.

What WAKR keeps on your phone

To work, WAKR stores the following on your phone. None of it is sent to us or to anyone else, and we can't see it.

  • Your alarms: their times, days, labels and sounds, whether they're on, and the wake-up task and length you chose.
  • Your wake-up history: when each alarm was due, when it rang and when it stopped; whether you finished the task, used the emergency exit or missed the alarm; how long you moved; and whether it was too dark for the camera. WAKR works out your streak from this.
  • A record of recent rings: for your last 60 alarms, the alarm's label, when it was due and when it rang, and whether your screen was on at the time. WAKR uses this to notice alarms that rang while the app was closed.
  • Your test alarm results: whether your screen was on, whether the alarm screen appeared, and your answer to "Did you hear it?".
  • Your setup: your answers to the setup questions, the plan you chose, and which setup steps you've completed.
  • Your settings, such as light or dark appearance.

WAKR also checks a few things on your phone, without storing or sending them: your phone's manufacturer (to show Samsung-only setup steps), whether its permissions and settings are on (to show what needs fixing), and your alarm volume (to warn you if it's low). If you tap Turn it up, WAKR raises your alarm volume.

The camera

WAKR uses your phone's camera to check that you're up and moving before your alarm stops, and during the practice run. The check runs entirely on your phone: it estimates where your body is in each camera frame, and how bright the image is, so it can tell whether you're moving and whether there's enough light. These estimates are used in the moment and then discarded. No photos, video or body measurements are saved, and nothing leaves your phone. WAKR doesn't identify you or recognize faces.

The camera is only on while the wake-up task or the practice run is on screen.

What appears on your lock screen

When an alarm rings, its label is shown on the alarm screen and in its notification, including over your lock screen. Anyone who sees your phone while it rings can see the label.

Permissions

WAKR asks for these permissions, for these reasons only:

  • Camera: for the wake-up task and the practice run, as described above.
  • Notifications: Android shows your alarm, including the alarm screen, through a notification.
  • Alarms and reminders: so your alarm rings at exactly the time you set.
  • Full-screen notifications: to show the alarm screen over your lock screen.
  • Running in the foreground: to keep your alarm ringing until you turn it off.
  • Running at startup: to set your alarms again after your phone restarts.
  • Vibration and keeping the phone awake: while your alarm rings.

WAKR also has Android's standard permissions to use the internet and to check your network connection. The current version doesn't send any information over the internet. WAKR doesn't use your location, microphone, contacts, photos or files.

WAKR may ask you to change some settings on your phone, such as battery optimization and Do Not Disturb, so your alarm isn't delayed or silenced. Changing them doesn't give WAKR any information. You can turn any permission off in your phone's settings, but WAKR may then be unable to wake you.

What we collect and share

Nothing. We don't collect, receive, sell or share personal information. WAKR has no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and you don't need an account to use it.

Google Play

You get WAKR from Google Play, which Google runs under the Google Privacy Policy. Google gives app developers combined statistics, such as install numbers, and anonymous crash reports from people who have chosen to share diagnostic data with Google. We use these only to find and fix problems.

Backups and new phones

If backup is turned on for your phone, Android may include WAKR's information in your backup to your Google Account, and copy it to a new phone when you move your data across. Google stores those backups under its own terms, not us. You can manage them in your phone's settings.

How long information is kept, and deleting it

Everything WAKR keeps stays on your phone:

  • Alarms stay until you delete them in WAKR.
  • The record of recent rings keeps only your last 60 alarms.
  • Test alarm results are replaced by each new test, and removed once WAKR has shown you the result.
  • Your wake-up history, setup answers and settings stay until you uninstall WAKR or clear its storage. WAKR doesn't have a way to delete these individually.

To delete everything, uninstall WAKR, or clear its storage in your phone's settings (Settings → Apps → WAKR → Storage). We hold no copy of your information, so there's nothing for us to access, correct, export or delete. It stays on your phone, under your control.

Security

WAKR's information is kept in its private storage on your phone, which Android doesn't let other apps read. WAKR doesn't add encryption of its own. Your phone's lock screen, and on phones that have it, built-in storage encryption, also protect it.

Children

WAKR is meant for people aged 13 and over. It isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't collect personal information from anyone, including children.

This website

This policy is published on a website hosted by GitHub Pages. The website has no cookies, analytics or tracking. GitHub logs visitors' IP addresses for security, under the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Changes to this policy

If WAKR starts sending any information off your phone, for example for subscriptions or an optional backup of your streak, we'll update this policy before that version of the app is released, and change the date at the top.
